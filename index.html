Privacy Policy

PlayTable  •  Effective date: October 29, 2026

PlayTable ("the app", "we", "us") is a collection of board, card and table games you can play against the device, with someone on the same phone, or with a nearby device (Chess, Checkers, Tic Tac Toe, Four in a Row, Dots & Boxes, Dominoes, Carrom, Table Soccer, Sea Battle, Air Hockey, Memory Match and others). This policy explains what the app collects, who it is shared with, and what is never collected.

Summary: PlayTable has no user accounts and never asks for your name, email, phone number or location. Your game history and settings stay on your device. The app shows ads and measures app activity: for that, it and the companies listed in Section 6 may use your device's advertising ID and records of what you do in the app. Where the law requires consent for personalised advertising, the app asks before any ad is requested, and you can change that answer at any time in Settings.

1. Data We Do Not Collect

The app has no mechanism to collect or receive any of the following:

  • Your name, email address, phone number, or other contact details
  • Any account or sign-in — the app has no user accounts
  • Your location. On Android 11 and below the app has to hold the location permission before the system will let it scan for Bluetooth devices, which is the only reason it is requested and is explained in Section 8. The app never asks the system where you are, and no location reaches us or anyone else.
  • Photos, camera, or microphone data
  • Contacts, calendar, SMS, call logs, or files on your device
  • Card numbers or bank details — see Section 5 on purchases

The display name you choose in the app is yours to invent; nothing checks it, and it is only sent to another device if you start a Nearby Play match (Section 7).

3. Advertising

PlayTable is free and shows ads. Ads are delivered through Google AdMob, and an individual ad may be filled by Google or by another ad network through AdMob mediation.

What is shared with ad networks

  • Advertising ID — the resettable device ID Android provides for ads
  • Approximate location — country or region, worked out from your IP address
  • Device and app info — device model, Android version, app version, language
  • Ad activity — which ads were requested, shown and tapped

Which networks

  • Google (AdMob)
  • Meta
  • Other ad networks taking part in AdMob mediation. The set changes over time; Google lists them at support.google.com/admob/answer/9012903, and we will tell you the current ones on request.

Your consent

Where the law requires it, the app shows Google's consent form before requesting any ad, and nothing above is shared until you answer it. Your answer is passed on to the networks. You can change it any time in Settings → Ad Privacy Options, and you can reset or delete your advertising ID for all apps in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads.

5. Purchases and Subscriptions

PlayTable offers an optional Premium Pro subscription that removes ads. Purchases are handled entirely by Google Play Billing:

  • Payment is taken by Google. The app never sees or stores your card, bank or billing details.
  • The app asks Google whether your account owns an active subscription and stores that answer, and the purchase token Google issues, on your device.
  • The fact that a subscription was bought, and its price and currency, is reported to Firebase Analytics and to Meta (Section 6) as a conversion event.
  • You can view, change or cancel the subscription in the Google Play Store, under Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions.

7. Nearby Play (Local Multiplayer)

Nearby Play lets two devices on the same Wi-Fi network, or within Bluetooth range of each other, connect directly to play a match. There is no server in between, and we never see any of it. When you use this feature:

  • Your chosen display name and the game you want to play are sent directly, device to device, to the opponent you connect with.
  • Your device likewise receives the opponent's display name and their moves during the match.
  • This exchange happens only over your local network or Bluetooth link, and only for the length of the session. It is not stored on any server, by us or anyone else, and is gone once the match ends.
  • While the Nearby Play screen is open, and only while it is open, the app broadcasts a short Bluetooth LE signal saying that a player here is looking for an opponent, and listens for the same signal from others. Both stop the moment you leave that screen, close the app, or turn Bluetooth off. Your phone is never made visible to Bluetooth devices in general.
  • That signal carries a random number identifying this installation and nothing else. It is not derived from your phone's hardware, it is not sent to us or to anyone but the device across the room, and it is used only to recognise that a player found over Wi-Fi and the same player found over Bluetooth are one person rather than two.
  • The app only sees devices running PlayTable: the filtering is done by the Bluetooth radio against this app's own identifier, so other phones, headsets and accessories nearby are never reported to it. It does not pair with anything, and it never connects to a device you have not chosen to invite or accept.

8. Permissions Used and Why

PermissionWhy it's needed
INTERNET Requesting ads, analytics and crash reports, checking your subscription with Google Play, and opening a direct connection to a nearby device for Nearby Play.
com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID Access to the device's resettable advertising ID, used by AdMob and Meta as described in Sections 3 and 6. Subject to your consent where consent is required.
com.android.vending.BILLING Required by Google Play to offer the Premium Pro subscription.
ACCESS_WIFI_STATE Checks whether Wi-Fi is on, so the app can find nearby opponents on the same network.
CHANGE_WIFI_MULTICAST_STATE Needed to discover nearby devices by multicast (mDNS) on your local network while Nearby Play is open.
BLUETOOTH, BLUETOOTH_ADMIN (Android 11 and below) Broadcasting and listening for the Nearby Play signal, and connecting to the opponent you pick, as an alternative to Wi-Fi.
ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION (Android 11 and below only) Android 11 and below have no separate Bluetooth scanning permission, so the system requires the location permission before any app may scan for Bluetooth devices. The app requests it for that reason alone: it never asks the system for your position, and nothing location-related is collected, stored or shared. Newer Android versions are never asked for it.
BLUETOOTH_CONNECT, BLUETOOTH_SCAN, BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE (Android 12+) The same purposes, using the permissions newer Android versions require: connecting to the opponent you pick, listening for nearby players, and broadcasting that you are looking for one. The scanning permission is declared with Android's neverForLocation flag, which tells the system the app must not derive your location from what it hears.
WRITE_SETTINGS Optional, and only on tablets: lets the app turn the screen to portrait so the board is not letterboxed. You grant it yourself on a system screen, and the app never changes any other setting. Declining it only means the app stays in whatever orientation the tablet is in.

The app does not request camera, microphone, contacts, storage, or SMS/call permissions, and does not request location on Android 12 or newer. Bluetooth is used only while the Nearby Play screen is open, only to find other people running this app and connect to the one you choose. The app never pairs with a device itself, and never makes your phone discoverable to Bluetooth devices in general.

9. Children's Privacy

PlayTable is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. Because the app shows advertising and uses an advertising ID, it is intended for a general audience rather than for children.

If you believe a child has used the app and you would like the associated advertising identifier dealt with, contact us at the address in Section 12. You can also clear the app's data or reset the device's advertising ID at any time, as described in Sections 3 and 10.

10. Your Choices and Data Deletion

  • Advertising consent: Settings → Ad Privacy Options, where the consent form applies to you.
  • Advertising ID: reset or delete it for all apps in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads.
  • App data on your device: use Reset Data in the app's Settings, or Android Settings → Apps → PlayTable → Storage → Clear Data.
  • Everything: uninstalling the app removes all data stored on your device.
  • Subscription: manage or cancel it in the Google Play Store.
  • Data already sent to Google or Meta: those companies hold it under their own policies, linked in Section 6, and each offers its own controls and deletion routes. We can ask them to delete data associated with your device on request — write to us at the address below.
